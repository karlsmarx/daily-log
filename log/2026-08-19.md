# 2026-08-19

Follow-up to yesterday's finding: rather than patching that one bypass and calling the locking design validated, ran a proper data-safety investigation into the single-writer SQLite architecture underneath it. Empirically compared write-ahead-log (WAL) vs. non-WAL behavior under concurrent load — throughput, and specifically what happens during the overlap window right after a restart, since that's when a writer is most likely to race a straggler from before the restart:

```sh
sqlite3 state.db 'PRAGMA journal_mode=WAL;'
sqlite3 state.db 'PRAGMA busy_timeout=5000;'
```

WAL mode plus a busy timeout removed most of the contention, but the restart-overlap question didn't have a clean empirical answer from a single-node test — reproducing it convincingly needs multiple writer processes racing across an actual restart, not simulated within one process. Wrote that up explicitly as an open item in the risk evaluation rather than rounding it off to "looks fine," since the whole point of the exercise was to find where the real edges of "single-writer" actually are.
